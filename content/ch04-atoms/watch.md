# Watch out

Places where the review sheet, slides or worksheets are wrong or easy to misread. Each starts with the deck it belongs to.

- (deck 1) The review sheet says the actinide series is named "because they follow the element actinide." The element is actinium (Ac, atomic number 89), as the periodic table on the slides shows. "Actinide" is the name of the series, not an element.
- (deck 1) Aluminum touches the metalloid staircase but is a metal. The review sheet calls this out; the slide's metalloids are B, Si, Ge, As, Sb and Te.
- (deck 1) The review sheet says there are 11 gases and 2 liquids at 25 °C but does not list them. Know the counts; if your teacher expects you to name them, check your notes.
- (deck 2) The guided notes spell the name "JJ Thompson." The slides spell it Thomson, which is correct.
- (deck 3) "Round the atomic mass to get the mass number" only works when no isotope is named. If the problem gives the isotope (carbon-13, Re-187, ⁴⁶Ti), use that mass number instead.
- (deck 5) The review sheet says "a positive atom is called a cation." A charged atom is an ion: a cation is a positive ion and an anion is a negative ion. A neutral atom has no charge.
- (deck 5) The review sheet lists charges for groups 1, 2, 13, 15, 16 and 17 but skips group 14. The slide's charge chart shows group 14 as 4+ or 4-, so it has no single charge to memorize.
- (deck 3) Long answer problem 4 asks about "Pb-297." Real lead isotopes are around Pb-204 to Pb-208, so this is probably a typo for Pb-207 (VERIFY with your teacher). The method is the same: lead always has 82 protons; neutrons = mass number - 82; a 2+ ion has 80 electrons.
