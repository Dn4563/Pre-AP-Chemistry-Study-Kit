# Watch out

Places where your sources disagree or the answer keys look wrong. Each starts with the deck it belongs to.

- (deck 3) Your Nomenclature Review key names Fe₂O₃ "iron(II) oxide." That's a mistake: 3 O²⁻ = 6−, split over 2 Fe = 3+, so it's iron(III) oxide (your study guide and binary worksheet both use iron(III) for this kind of formula).
- (deck 3) The review key names AgN "silver nitride." Silver is always 1+ and nitride is 3−, so silver nitride would be Ag₃N. The formula in the review is probably a typo. VERIFY.
- (deck 4) The review key's "trinitrogen decachloride N₂C10" and "pentasulfur heptabromide S5Br" are scanning errors: by the prefixes they are N₃Cl₁₀ and S₅Br₇.
- (deck 6) The review key names "H₃N" hydronitric acid. NH₃ is normally called ammonia, and it doesn't act like the acids in your notes. If it comes up, ask your teacher. VERIFY.
- (deck 5) The review key calls ZnHPO₄ "zinc biphosphate." The ion HPO₄²⁻ is hydrogen phosphate (your practice test uses "aluminum hydrogen phosphate" for Al₂(HPO₄)₃). Use the name on your ion sheet.
- (deck 5) Your study guide accepts both "bicarbonate" and "hydrogen carbonate" for HCO₃⁻.
- (deck 3) The review key writes copper(I) as "copper (1)" and iron(III) as "Iron (111)" in places: those are Roman numerals I and III.
- (deck 5) Polyatomic ion names come from your class sheet on the test. The table in the Study guide lists only the ions that appear in your worksheets; if your sheet uses a different name, use the sheet's.
- (deck 1) The guided notes also mention "hyposulfite" as an example oxyanion but never give its formula, so it's left out.
