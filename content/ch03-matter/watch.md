# Watch out

Notes where the slides, review sheet or textbook disagree or are easy to misread. Each starts with the deck it belongs to.

- (deck 5) In the review sheet's colloid FRQ the Structure column lists "Solid in a Gas" and "Liquid in a Gas" twice. The teacher said in class that this was a mistake. The two repeats should be "Solid in a Liquid" (sol: paint, cell fluid) and "Liquid in a Solid" (gel: jelly, cheese). Aerosol and solid sol really are each used twice, exactly as on her colloid reference sheet.
- (deck 5) The lab sheet lists 3% hydrogen peroxide in the materials but Station 6 says (30%) hydrogen peroxide and "extremely dangerous." Treat it as a hazardous material either way; the chemical change (peroxide reacting with the iron nail) is the same.
- (deck 1) The review asks how water can be all three states at 0 °C. Ice and liquid water coexist at 0 °C, and water vapor is also present above them, so say all three explicitly and name the change that makes the vapor.
- (deck 2) The review sheet lists pH as a chemical property (along with reacting or not reacting). In the Ada Twist FRQ treat pH and "reacts with bases and metals" as chemical properties, and thick, clear, smell and density as physical.
- (deck 1) The teacher's notes sheet for Section 1 garbles the states of matter ("deficient" and "indeficient" space and volume). The correct version: a solid has a fixed shape and volume, a liquid has a fixed volume but takes the shape of its container, and a gas has neither a fixed shape nor a fixed volume.
- (deck 2) The slides list "rusting of steel" as a chemical property. Rusting itself is a chemical change; the chemical property is the ability to rust. Treat both as chemical on a test.
- (deck 2) The teacher's intensive/extensive example (drops of food coloring changing how intense the water's color looks) is really about the amount of dye. The same notes list color as intensive. The color of a pure substance is intensive; know the notes' example if it is asked.
- (deck 2) The row "Indicators of a Chemical Change" is blank in the notes framework. This kit uses the five signs from the teacher's review sheet: precipitate, gas given off, color change, temperature change and light given off. Odor is a physical property, so it is not used as a sign.
- (deck 3) The answer key in the Classification of Matter activity lists "Drain Cleaner (Al & NaOH & ...)" as a compound, but any code with an & is a mixture. Use the & rule on a test.
- (deck 3) A subscript does not make something a compound: Br₂, P₄ and O₃ are elements. Atom does not equal element and molecule does not equal compound.
- (deck 4) Dissolving is a physical change even though the solid seems to disappear. That is why salt water can be separated by distillation with no new substances formed.
- (deck 5) Station 6 lists 3% hydrogen peroxide in the materials list but 30% in the procedure. Follow your teacher's safety instructions.
- (deck 5) The colloid reference sheet is extra and is not in the teacher's slides. The chapter treats mixtures as homogeneous (solutions) or heterogeneous.
